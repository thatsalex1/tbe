What If There Is a Disagreement?

By the LegacyHold Team · 5 min read

Most escrow deals close without a hitch: the buyer funds escrow, the seller delivers, the buyer confirms, and the crypto releases. But deals between strangers occasionally go sideways — the item doesn't match the description, the service was never finished, or the seller simply goes quiet. That's exactly the situation escrow is built to handle.

What happens when you open a dispute

  1. Either side can raise it. As long as the funds are still in escrow, either the buyer or the seller can flag that something's wrong — directly from the transaction in their dashboard.
  2. Funds are frozen immediately. The moment a dispute opens, the escrowed crypto is locked. Neither side can force a release while it's under review.
  3. Both sides submit evidence. See the table below for what actually helps.
  4. Our team reviews it. We look at the evidence from both sides against the original terms of the deal.
  5. A decision is made. The crypto is released to the seller, refunded to the buyer, or in some cases split, depending on what the evidence supports.

What counts as useful evidence

Evidence typeWhy it helps
On-chain transaction hashProves exactly when and how much was sent to escrow
Chat logs from the dealShows what was actually agreed, not just remembered
Photos or screen recordingsDocuments the condition of goods, a deliverable, or a service outcome
Tracking or delivery confirmationEstablishes whether and when something was actually sent

The more specific and timestamped your evidence is, the faster we can resolve the case.

How long it takes

Straightforward cases with clear on-chain and photo evidence are usually resolved within 24 to 48 hours. Cases that need more back-and-forth between both parties can take longer. Either way, your crypto stays safely frozen in the multi-sig escrow wallet for the entire review — it can't be moved by either party while a dispute is open.

Good to know: If a seller never responds or fails to prove they delivered, the buyer's crypto is refunded in full. Escrow exists precisely so one side going silent doesn't cost you your funds.

How to avoid needing a dispute at all

  • Write out exactly what's being exchanged, including specifics, before funding escrow.
  • Keep all communication about the deal on the platform, where it's part of the record.
  • Take photos or screen recordings of a deliverable the moment it's handed over.
  • Don't confirm receipt until you've actually verified everything matches what was agreed.

Trade knowing a fair process has your back

Every deal is protected by the same dispute process, whether it's your first or your fiftieth.
